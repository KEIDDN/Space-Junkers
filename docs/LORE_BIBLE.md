# Space Junkers: lore bible

For consistency, not for the player. The player gets this in fragments: a line from Fedya, a
note in a locker, a roster with a name crossed out. Nothing in the game should contradict this
page; nothing in the game should explain all of it.

## The premise in one breath

Forty-one years ago every clock in the Commonwealth stopped on the same thousandth of a second
and the orders stopped coming. Nobody came to say why. The colonies waited, then left, then
started taking each other apart. Five people keep a fifty-year-old ore tender flying by going
down into what was left behind and bringing up what still works. You are the newest of them.

The story is not "what happened to the Commonwealth". The story is five people trying to keep
a ship running in a universe that has mostly moved on without them.

## The world is its own thing

The Commonwealth is fictional. Its visual language (stencils, Cyrillic labels, brutalist
concrete, analogue dials, tractor plants) is retro-industrial state design, not a statement
about any real country. Real-world history, rulers and politics never appear. Names are the
Commonwealth's working language; that is all.

## Timeline

Crew count years from the Blackout ("year forty-one"); most round it to "forty years".

| When | What | What's left of it |
|---|---|---|
| Before | The Commonwealth expands from Earth ("Central") into the **Otets** system: a gas giant and its moons and neighbours. Ore, tractors, garrisons, listening posts. Built to last a hundred years; it lasted about sixty out here. | Every facility, every stencil, the kosmoruble. |
| The Thinning (last ~20 years before) | Fewer ships from Central every year. Quotas keep arriving; spare parts don't. People mend things with boots and tape. | Maintenance tickets, requisitions with "NO RESPONSE (x19)". |
| **Year 0: the Blackout** | 00:00:00.000. Every networked clock stops on the same instant. Central stops answering. No war, no speech. The Blackout Protocol says: seal the vaults, keep the listening watch, don't answer channel nine. | Stopped clocks. Sealed red doors. Roll calls that never end. |
| Years 0–9: the Wait | Garrisons hold. Plants keep producing for nobody. Payroll fails every morning. A "message from Central" arrives nine days late with only the date changed. | Shift logs, payroll errors, Depot 4 still holding. |
| Years 9–20: the Leaving | Supply ships stop entirely. Whoever can, leaves for the Belt. Stations are evacuated, some in minutes. Hospitals choose who gets the last antibiotics. | Meals left on desks. Children's drawings in lockers. "They evacuated this place in six minutes." |
| Years 20–41: the Salvage | The Belt markets become the economy. Kombinat Holdings buys station title at the Receivership auctions. Squatters live in the plants. Garrison children inherit their parents' oath. Independent crews raid. | Everything the player sees. |

What happened to Earth: **nobody knows, and the crew disagree.** Fedya thinks Earth is fine and
simply forgot them. Shura thinks the network was switched off from outside, and that channel
nine has something to do with it. Doc thinks it doesn't matter. Molot won't discuss it. Lis sells
three different versions depending on the buyer. All of them can be partly right.

## How the present works

- **The economy.** The Belt markets buy anything that still works: cells, wire, drives, medicine,
  metal. Kosmorubli (КР) are still the money because nobody has printed anything better. Fuel is
  the real cost of everything.
- **Why facilities aren't stripped.** There are thousands of them, most are far, and fuel is
  expensive. The deep sections were sealed by protocol and stay sealed while the reserve cells
  hold. When a station's reserve finally fails, its seals fail with it and doors that held for
  forty years open: a fresh "signal". Crews race for those. The Lastochka's nav console lists
  them by signal number; that is why every raid is somewhere new.
- **Why some doors are jammed.** Crews weld shutters behind them to keep the next crew off
  their backs, or to keep something in. Forcing one is quick and very loud.
- **Why a seal can fail while you're inside.** Reserve cells die when they die. Shura keeps a
  log of when; she has noticed they fail on the minute, never between. Nobody explains that.
- **Why it's dangerous.** Squatters (scavengers) live in the plants and treat them as home.
  Raider crews got there first. On Krasnaya the garrison's children still hold the depots under
  orders nobody countermanded. On Kombinat, corporate security guards property they don't
  understand.
- **Who controls what.** Nobody controls the system. Kombinat Holdings owns paper. Garrisons own
  what they can shoot. Squatters own the rooms they sleep in. The Lastochka owns nothing but
  herself, and Fedya owes money on her.
- **Why contracts exist.** They are not a quest board. They are the crew's own needs (a pump
  part, dressings, a drive Shura has wanted for years) and the occasional buyer on the Belt who
  wants something from a particular place. What can be bought aboard is what already circulates;
  what must be raided is specific, fresh, site-bound or never for sale.
- **Why the ship needs money.** Fuel, parts, Fedya's creditor on the Belt, and food.

## The threads (what the records slowly suggest)

1. **The Blackout**: every clock agrees on the second; networks don't fail like that.
2. **Channel Nine**: every node had a receiver on nine; you were never to answer it.
3. **Those who stayed**: the workers nobody told to go home.
4. **Why the lights are on**: when there's almost no power, it goes to the listening post first.
5. **What happened**: the Commonwealth didn't fall. It stopped.

Sirin is where the threads converge. It stays unexplained.

## Tone

Dry, understated, cynical, occasionally warm, occasionally brutal. Funny because life is terrible,
not because the game is a joke. People say short things. Nobody explains their personality.

## Dialogue classes

Every line is one of:

- **FUNCTIONAL**: what the player must know to play. Plain, specific, keys named. (Radio coach,
  contract objectives and their `intel` line, prompts.)
- **CHARACTER**: who this person is. (Greetings, reactions, most chatter.)
- **LORE**: something about the world. May be mysterious. (Records, notes, topics.)
- **ATMOSPHERE**: flavour. Doesn't need to be understood.

Never hide functional information inside lore. Contract briefs are character; the objective
text and `intel` under them are functional.
